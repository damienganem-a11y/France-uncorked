The journey

Land in Paris, and by lunch your glass is already sparkling.

We skip the city and go straight to the vineyards. From there, France unfolds south one region at a time: the bubbles of Champagne, the wild green hills of the Jura, the food capital of Lyon and its legendary Rhône wines, and finally the light and the stones of Provence.

Chapter one

Champagne

Chalk cellars, Grand Cru villages and a first night among the vines.

Tap a word to discover its story

This is where the bubbles begin. We go beyond the famous labels to the families who grow their own grapes, some of the most exciting winemakers in France, and two nights in two very special houses. The first in the Selosse family home, among the vines of the Côte des Blancs. The second in Reims, at the three-star house that opens the doors of Krug.

01Arrival

Straight into the vines

Your driver waits at Charles de Gaulle, and less than two hours later you're in Avize, at Les Avisés, the house of cult winemaker Anselme Selosse. Time to rest, a slow walk through the Grand Cru vines, and a first tasting with a grower in the village.

Tonight

Dinner at the long table of Les Avisés, with Selosse champagnes poured as they are meant to be.

In your glass Selosse Initial, Blanc de Blancs from the family's own Grand Cru vines.
02Bubbles

Grand Cru villages, Krug and three stars

A morning with the growers of Cramant and Le Mesnil, then on to Reims. In the afternoon, Krug opens its doors for a private visit, the most exclusive house in Champagne, followed by the cathedral where the kings of France were crowned.

Tonight

L'Assiette Champenoise, Arnaud Lallement's three Michelin stars.

In your glass Krug Grande Cuvée, then Krug Vintage and Krug Rosé with dinner.

Chapter two

Jura

France's best-kept wine secret, and the heart of natural wine.

Green hills, forests, cliff-top villages, and winemakers who still do everything by hand. This is where vin jaune ages for six years under a veil of yeast, where comté sleeps in mountain forts, and the region natural wine lovers in Napa swear by. We stay in an 18th-century château by the river and let the region surprise you.

03Road south

Through Burgundy, into the hills

A last champagne in the morning, then a beautiful drive south with a long lunch in Chablis (or Beaune, if Burgundy calls you). By late afternoon, the château gates open.

Tonight

Dinner at the château, first sips of Savagnin.

In your glass Premier Cru Chablis with lunch, then a first fresh Savagnin.
04Vin jaune

Arbois, Pupillin and Château-Chalon

Natural winemakers in Arbois and the tiny village of Pupillin, with a stop at Louis Pasteur's family home. After lunch, the cliff-top village of Château-Chalon and its legendary vin jaune, then Fort Saint-Antoine, where 100,000 wheels of comté age inside a mountain fort.

Tonight

A candlelit dinner back at the château.

In your glass Vin jaune from Château-Chalon, with a 24-month comté.

Chapter three

Lyon & the Rhône

Steep Syrah terraces, Beaujolais rebels and the food capital of France.

Lyon is where French gastronomy was born, and for three nights it's home. To the south, the dramatic slopes of Côte-Rôtie and Hermitage and lunch at Maison Pic. To the north, the Beaujolais villages where the natural wine movement started, and dinner at the legendary Paul Bocuse. In between, markets, Roman ruins and the best bistros in the world.

05Lyon

Hello, Lyon

A last Jura tasting on the way, then Lyon. We get lost in the hidden passageways of the old town and climb to Fourvière for the view over a sea of red roofs.

Tonight

A real Lyon bouchon, red-checked tablecloths and all.

In your glass A Morgon or Fleurie, the house wine of every good bouchon.

Tonight

Free to wander, or a glass by the river.

In your glass Côte-Rôtie and Condrieu, the two faces of the Northern Rhône.

Chapter four

Provence

Galets, Grenache, lavender skies and a village all of our own.

An hour on the fast train and the light changes. We finish in a hilltop village facing Mont Ventoux, close to the vineyards of Châteauneuf-du-Pape, where the vines grow through big round stones that hold the sun's heat into the night. Long lunches, perched villages, and one last dinner under the stars.

08South

Into the sun

First class on the TGV, and an hour later you step out in Provence. Lunch in Châteauneuf-du-Pape under the ruins of the Popes' summer castle, then a walk through vineyards covered in galets.

Tonight

Dinner on the terrace, Mont Ventoux turning pink at sunset.

In your glass Châteauneuf-du-Pape, poured where the vines grow through the stones.
09Galets

Gigondas, then in Van Gogh's footsteps

The jagged limestone Dentelles above Gigondas and a village lunch in Séguret. In the afternoon we head south to Saint-Rémy-de-Provence and Saint-Paul de Mausole, the old monastery where Van Gogh lived in 1889 and painted The Starry Night. Then, just outside its walls, a private painting class among the olive trees and cypresses he painted, with easels, paints and a glass of rosé waiting for you.

Tonight

Our farewell dinner, and a very special bottle.

In your glass Gigondas, a chilled rosé at the easel, and a very special farewell bottle.
10À bientôt

A last coffee, then home

A slow breakfast in the village, then we ride the direct train together to Charles de Gaulle. I walk you all the way to check-in and see your bags off, so the only thing left to do is board with your heads full of stories.

Good to know

Before you ask

What is the pace like?

Relaxed. Usually two visits a day, long lunches, and free time in the late afternoon. Every day can bend to your mood.

How much walking?

Easy walks through villages and vineyards. Comfortable shoes are enough. The steep Côte-Rôtie terraces are optional.

Is there a dress code?

Smart casual during the day. For the three-star dinners, think elegant: a jacket for men is welcome, no tie needed.

What about allergies or diets?

Tell me once, and every restaurant and winemaker knows before you arrive.

Do we need to speak French?

Not a word. Everything happens in English, and I translate with the winemakers who don't.

Can we change things?

Absolutely. More Krug, a spa afternoon, a cooking class, a slower morning: this is your journey.

Shall we open the first bottle?

Spring in France is short and the best cellars book early. Tell me when and who's coming, and I'll start opening doors.
